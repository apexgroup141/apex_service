Helpful heat pump information

One Heat Pump for Heating and Cooling Your Home

A heat pump is an HVAC system that can heat your home in winter and cool it in summer. During the summer, it works much like a central air conditioner, moving heat from inside the home to the outdoors. In winter, the process reverses: the system collects available heat from the outdoor air and transfers it into the home.

The key difference between a heat pump and electric resistance heating is that a heat pump moves heat rather than creating it directly. This allows heat pumps to provide efficient home heating while also serving as the home's central cooling system during the summer.

Heat pumps are especially well suited to the Puget Sound climate. Our winters are relatively mild compared with many colder parts of the country, and modern heat pumps are designed to operate at temperatures well below those we typically experience here. A properly sized heat pump can fully meet a home's heating needs under our regional design conditions while using the same system for cooling during the summer.

Heat pumps also offer flexibility in how a new HVAC system is configured. A fully electric system can pair a heat pump with an air handler to provide both heating and cooling. If a homeowner wants to keep an existing natural gas or propane furnace, a dual-fuel configuration is also possible. In that setup, the heat pump remains the primary source of heat, while the furnace is retained as a backup heat source when needed.

In many homes with an existing central A/C system and ductwork, upgrading to a heat pump may be possible without rebuilding the entire air-distribution system, provided the existing ductwork has the appropriate size, condition and airflow for the new equipment.

Depending on the equipment selected, heat pumps can also provide quiet operation, high energy efficiency, more consistent indoor comfort and modern system controls. More advanced models can automatically adjust their capacity to match the home's current heating or cooling demand, although these capabilities vary by equipment class and model.

Overall, a heat pump combines heating and air conditioning in one system and is particularly well suited to providing year-round comfort in the Puget Sound climate.

What You Get When You Choose Apex Service Group

When you choose us for your heat pump installation, our work goes beyond simply replacing equipment. We first evaluate how the existing HVAC system operates so the new system can be selected around the actual conditions of the home, not just the capacity of the old equipment.

When replacing an existing system, we may check supply and return static pressure, airflow distribution and the condition of the ductwork. This helps identify restrictions that could affect comfort, efficiency or the performance of the new heat pump.

We also discuss your preferences for the location of the outdoor and indoor equipment, thermostat, refrigerant lines and other system components. Whenever technically possible and consistent with manufacturer requirements and proper system operation, we work to accommodate those preferences during installation.

The same specialist who evaluates the home and participates in selecting the equipment remains directly involved in the installation. This helps ensure that important details identified during the initial visit are not lost between a salesperson and a separate installation crew.

We guarantee professional, high-quality installation performed in accordance with applicable manufacturer requirements, followed by proper system setup and commissioning.

After installation, we verify the operation of the complete system, including airflow, controls, temperatures, electrical operating parameters and other key measurements. A complete report with the main system operating parameters is then emailed to the homeowner.

We provide a 10-year labor warranty on qualifying installation work, and the installed equipment is also covered by the applicable manufacturer warranty.
