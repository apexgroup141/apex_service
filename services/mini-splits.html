Why Mini-Splits Work So Well—and Why Installation Matters

Ductless mini-split systems provide efficient heating and cooling without the need for traditional ductwork. They offer quiet operation, individual temperature control for different areas of the home, and excellent flexibility for bedrooms, additions, ADUs, converted spaces, and entire homes.

However, even high-quality equipment can only perform as well as it is installed. Proper system sizing, indoor unit placement, refrigerant piping, condensate drainage, evacuation, and final setup all affect comfort, efficiency, noise levels, reliability, and equipment life. In many cases, the quality of the installation matters more than the brand name or purchase price alone.

Mini-split questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a mini split installation cost?

Typical starting ranges are $3,000–$7,000 for a single-zone system, $4,500–$11,000 for two zones, $5,500–$13,000 for three zones, and $7,000 or more for larger systems. Final cost depends on equipment, capacity, electrical work, line-set length, permits and installation conditions.

Can a mini split heat my home in winter?

Many modern ductless heat pumps can provide effective winter heating, but performance varies by model, outdoor temperature, system sizing and the home’s heating load. We evaluate those factors before recommending equipment.

Do I need electrical work for a mini split?

Mini-splits require an appropriate dedicated electrical connection. The existing panel, circuit capacity, disconnect location and equipment requirements determine whether additional electrical work is needed.

Do mini split installations require permits?

Requirements depend on the project and local jurisdiction. Apex handles required permits when applicable.

What is the difference between single-zone and multi-zone mini splits?

A single-zone system connects one indoor unit to one outdoor unit. A multi-zone system connects multiple indoor units to compatible outdoor equipment, allowing separate comfort zones. The better option depends on the layout, loads and how the rooms are used.

How long does a typical mini split installation take?

Many straightforward single-zone installations can be completed in one working day. Multi-zone systems, electrical changes, difficult line routing or other project conditions can require more time.

How do I know how many indoor units I need?

We assess the room layout, heating and cooling load, doors, airflow paths and how each space is used. That determines whether one indoor unit, multiple zones or another system layout makes sense.
