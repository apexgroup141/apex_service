Seattle homes

What usually shapes a Seattle heating and cooling plan.

Seattle housing stock is older than most of the region: early-1900s Craftsmans and bungalows, mid-century homes with oil furnaces, and plenty of houses heated by baseboards or wall heaters. That changes what a good proposal looks like.

  • Oil furnace conversions. Many homes still heat with an oil tank. A ducted heat pump can often reuse the existing ducts; the old tank needs to be decommissioned by a licensed tank service.
  • Baseboard and wall-heater homes. No ducts usually means a ductless or multi-zone system, like the two-zone Daikin install above. Keeping a few baseboards in rarely used rooms is sometimes the most sensible budget. Compare heat pumps vs. baseboard heat.
  • Tight lots and close neighbors. Outdoor unit placement, line-set routes and noise matter on narrow city lots. Quiet inverter equipment and careful placement are part of the design, not an afterthought.
  • Electrical capacity. Older panels may need a load calculation or an upgrade before a heat pump is added.
  • Summer heat. Seattle homes built without AC are increasingly uncomfortable in July and August. A heat pump adds cooling with the same equipment that heats.

Not sure whether your existing ducts can carry a heat pump? Start with can your ducts support a heat pump and ductless vs. central air.

Seattle FAQ

Common questions from Seattle homeowners.

Do you work throughout Seattle?

Yes. We schedule installation, maintenance and repair work across Seattle, including West Seattle and South Seattle, and on the Eastside in Bellevue, Kirkland and Redmond. Larger installs are planned ahead, so ask about scheduling when you request an estimate.

Can I replace an oil furnace with a heat pump?

Usually, yes. If the ducts are in reasonable shape, a ducted heat pump can replace the oil furnace; if not, a ductless or multi-zone system can heat the main living areas. The oil tank must be decommissioned or removed separately.

What if my Seattle home only has baseboard heat?

A ductless mini-split or multi-zone heat pump is the most common upgrade, because it does not need ducts. Our Daikin two-zone project is a Seattle example of exactly that conversion.

Do mini-splits need regular cleaning?

Yes. Washable filters should be cleaned regularly, and indoor units benefit from a professional deep cleaning when the coil or blower wheel collects buildup. See our Seattle deep cleaning project for what that involves.
