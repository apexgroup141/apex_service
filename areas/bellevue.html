What usually shapes an Eastside heating and cooling plan.

Much of Bellevue, Kirkland, Redmond and Sammamish is 1970s–2000s housing with a gas furnace, ductwork and often no air conditioning, alongside newer, tighter homes that need planned ventilation. That makes the Eastside a strong fit for ducted heat pumps.

  • Gas furnace to heat pump. When the existing ducts are sized well, a heat pump and air handler can replace the furnace and add cooling in one project, as in our Kirkland replacement.
  • Hybrid systems. Some homeowners keep a gas furnace as backup heat and add a heat pump for most of the year. We compare both paths in writing.
  • Adding AC to a ducted home. Homes built without AC can often add ducted cooling or a heat pump through the existing duct system.
  • Fresh air in tight homes. Newer and remodeled homes may benefit from an HRV that brings in filtered fresh air while recovering heat, like the Bellevue HRV install.
  • Quiet, tidy exteriors. Outdoor unit placement, line-hide covers and noise are planned up front, which matters in neighborhoods with HOA rules and close neighbors.

Eastside FAQ

Common questions from Bellevue and Eastside homeowners.

Which Eastside cities do you serve?

We schedule installation, maintenance and repair work in Bellevue, Kirkland, Redmond, Sammamish, Issaquah, Mercer Island, Newcastle and Bothell. Larger installs are planned ahead, so ask about scheduling when you request an estimate.

Can a heat pump use my existing furnace ductwork?

Often, yes. A heat pump usually needs more airflow than a furnace, so we check duct sizing and return air first. In our Kirkland project, the new air handler connected to the existing ducts with fitted transitions.

Should I keep my gas furnace as backup?

It depends on the home, the furnace's age and your goals. A full heat pump removes gas heating; a hybrid system keeps the furnace for the coldest hours. Running costs can differ between the two, so we compare them in writing.

What is an HRV and do I need one?

A heat recovery ventilator brings in filtered outdoor air and exhausts stale air while recovering most of the heat. It is most useful in tight, newer or heavily remodeled homes where moisture, odors or stuffiness build up.
