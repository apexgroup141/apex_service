Gym Ventilation with a Fantech ERV in North Bend, WA

A workout space needs a steady supply of fresh air and a reliable way to remove stale, humid air. For this gym in North Bend, Apex Service Group planned the layout, installed a Fantech energy recovery ventilator with its own supply and extract duct runs, and then measured and balanced the airflow before handing the system over.

Location
North Bend, WA
Space
Gym, approx. 28 × 40 ft
Equipment
Fantech ATMO 300E-EC ERV
Airflow
Approx. 130–340 CFM

Project overview

Fresh air for a gym, planned before the first duct went up.

The client wanted dependable fresh air ventilation in a large, open gym with a high steel-frame ceiling and two garage doors. During a workout, people add a lot of heat and moisture to the air, so the room needed continuous air exchange rather than an open door or a single exhaust fan.

Starting from the client's sketch, we prepared a ventilation layout: a Fantech ATMO 300E-EC ERV mounted at the ceiling, a fresh air supply run on one side of the room, a room air extract run on the other, and separate exterior connections for outdoor intake and exhaust.

Installation scope

Details that make a ventilation system work as designed

01

Grille Placement by Height

  • Placed the fresh air supply grilles at 12 ft.
  • Placed the extract grilles at 14 ft, near the ceiling, where warm and humid air collects.
  • Kept to the planned grille heights throughout the room.
02

8-Inch Ductwork

  • Ran 8-inch round duct instead of 6-inch.
  • The larger duct moves the same air at lower velocity, with less resistance and less air noise.
  • Gives the ERV room to deliver its higher speed settings.
03

A Damper at Every Grille

  • Installed a separate balancing damper for each individual grille.
  • Lets the airflow be divided evenly between the supply and extract points.
  • Allows fine adjustment later without opening up the ductwork.
04

Condensation Control & Drainage

  • Separately insulated part of the incoming outdoor air duct to minimize condensation.
  • Built the condensate drain from rigid PVC pipe.
  • Rigid pipe holds its slope and does not sag the way a flexible drain line can.

Commissioning & balancing

Measured airflow, not guesswork.

Once the installation was complete, we set up and balanced the Fantech ERV airflow:

  • Minimum speed: approximately 130–140 CFM.
  • Maximum speed: approximately 320–340 CFM.

We set the supply airflow about 5–10% higher than the exhaust. This creates slight positive pressure in the gym while the system runs, which helps reduce dusty outside air being drawn in through gaps around the doors and other leaks in the building.

Balancing was done by adjusting the supply and exhaust fan speeds. Total airflow was measured with a differential manometer and read against the manufacturer's airflow table. We also measured the air velocity in each duct separately and adjusted the distribution with the mechanical dampers.

Final result

Continuous fresh air the client can control from the wall.

The gym now has a balanced fresh air system that brings in outdoor air, removes stale and humid room air, and recovers energy between the two air streams. The client controls the ventilation speed from the Fantech wall control, from a low continuous setting to full boost during heavy use.

Planning the layout first, sizing the ducts generously, placing a damper at every grille and measuring the airflow at the end are what turn a set of ducts into a ventilation system that performs as designed. For more on fresh air systems, read our whole-house ventilation guide.
